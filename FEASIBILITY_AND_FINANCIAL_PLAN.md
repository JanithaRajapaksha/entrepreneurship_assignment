## 4. Planned Structure for Section 4: Feasibility Study

Section 4 must strictly follow the four pillars taught in Chapter 03 of the course curriculum:

### 4.1 Section 4.A — Product / Service Feasibility Analysis
* **What needs to be added:**
  1. **Concept Statement & Desirability Testing:**
     * Draft the formal 5-part Concept Statement (Product/Service Idea, Target Customer Group, Unique Value Proposition, Special Technical Features, Management Team Profile).
     * Summarize feedback from a panel of 20–30 potential users evaluating whether the concept makes sense and solves a genuine daily problem.
  2. **Product/Service Demand & Primary Survey Validation:**
     * Integrate quantitative data from the Google Forms survey.
     * Tabulate the **Buying Intentions Survey** (using the standard 5-point scale: *Definitely would use*, *Probably would use*, *Neutral*, *Unlikely*, *Definitely not*).
     * Apply industry-standard discount factors (50% to "Definitely", 10%–20% to "Probably") to calculate a statistically realistic base demand rather than naive stated interest.
     * Qualitative "Gumshoe Research" summary: Insights from direct discussions with corporate employees and salon professionals in Colombo.

### 4.2 Section 4.B — Industry and Target Market Feasibility Analysis
* **What needs to be added:**
  1. **Industry Attractiveness (Chapter 04):**
     * Analysis of the Sri Lankan personal care / salon service industry (growth trajectory, post-crisis consumer spending shifts, operational costs).
     * **Porter's Five Forces Model Matrix:**
       * *Rivalry among competitors* (Moderate: fixed salons exist, but mobile competitors are absent).
       * *Threat of new entrants* (Moderate-High: low capital barriers for imitators once proven).
       * *Bargaining power of buyers* (Moderate-High: discretionary personal service; price sensitive).
       * *Bargaining power of suppliers* (Low-Moderate: vehicles, plumbing, hair products are widely available).
       * *Threat of substitutes* (High: washing hair at home, domestic helpers, traditional salons).
  2. **Target Market Attractiveness:**
     * Evaluation of high-density corporate employment hubs: **World Trade Center (WTC)**, **Havelock City / Mireka Tower**, **Orion City**, and **Access Towers**.
     * Comparative evaluation matrix assessing parking permissions, corporate density, power access, and footfall.

### 4.3 Section 4.C — Organizational and Operational Feasibility Analysis
* **What needs to be revised & expanded:**
  1. **Management Prowess:**
     * Profile the founding team (engineering undergraduates possessing electrical, systems, and project management competencies).
     * Identify missing competencies (professional hair dressing, commercial accounting) and how they are filled via skilled hiring or external advisors.
  2. **Resource Sufficiency (The 6–12 Critical Non-Financial Resources):**
     * Explicitly evaluate the availability of: (1) Suitable commercial vehicle chassis; (2) Qualified mobile vehicle bodybuilders; (3) Professional salon technicians; (4) Reliable high-volume freshwater source; (5) Licensed municipal wastewater discharge point; (6) Reserved high-demand corporate parking slots; (7) RMV vehicle modification approval; (8) Local health/sanitation clearance; (9) Mobile booking software / WhatsApp gateway; (10) Digital marketing channels.
  3. **Engineering & Operational Feasibility (Retaining & Upgrading Existing Strengths):**
     * Detailed layout and ergonomic plan for the vehicle interior.
     * Service turnaround cycle (e.g., 20-minute washing & quick dry, 5-minute cleaning protocol).
     * Queue and digital appointment management.
     * Daily throughput and vehicle capacity constraints (calculating theoretical vs. practical maximum appointments).

### 4.4 Section 4.D — Preliminary Financial Feasibility Summary
* Summarize capital requirements, unit economics, and operational break-even as a bridge to the full financial modeling in Section 7.
* Conclude with a clear **Feasibility Scorecard** (High / Medium / Low risk across all 4 pillars) and an explicit **Go / Conditional Go / Pivot** recommendation.

## 5. Planned Structure for Section 7: Financial Projection & Funding

This is currently the biggest gap in `feasibility_study_draft.tex` (which currently has only two preliminary tables and a single formula). Section 7 must be expanded into a rigorous financial and engineering economics chapter:

### 5.1 Section 7.1 — Comprehensive Startup Capital Investment (CapEx)
* **Detailed Itemized Budget:**
  1. *Vehicle Acquisition:* Outright purchase vs. down payment on commercial lease (e.g., 20%–30% down payment on a used Isuzu Elf / Mitsubishi Canter).
  2. *Custom Vehicle Conversion:* Insulation, waterproof flooring, wall paneling, partitioned service bays, cabinetry, plumbing integration.
  3. *Equipment & System Installation:*
     * Salon equipment (ergonomic shampoo basins, reclining chairs, high-velocity hair dryers, styling mirrors, towel warmers).
     * Electrical power system (hybrid inverter 3–5 kVA, deep-cycle lithium/AGM auxiliary battery bank, roof-mounted solar PV array, shore power hookup cable).
     * Water & wastewater system (food-grade clean water tank, baffles, dual 12V diaphragm pumps, low-flow aerated fixtures, baffled greywater retention tank).
  4. *Regulatory, Licensing & Setup:* RMV inspection & modification endorsement, trade licenses, public liability insurance.
  5. *Initial Working Capital:* Cash cushion covering 3 months of fixed operating expenses.
  6. *Contingency Allowance:* 10%–15% reserve for unexpected fabrication or mechanical contingencies.

### 5.2 Section 7.2 — Monthly Operating Expenditure (OpEx) Breakdown
* Categorize expenses into strict **Fixed Costs** and **Variable Costs**:
  * **Fixed Operating Costs:**
    * Staff salaries (Driver/facilities operator + 2 professional female salon stylists + EPF/ETF statutory contributions).
    * Vehicle commercial insurance, road tax, and licensing amortization.
    * Base marketing and digital booking software subscription.
    * Scheduled vehicle and equipment preventative maintenance reserve.
    * Parking / facility access fees at target corporate hubs.
  * **Variable Operating Costs (Per-Customer / Per-Day):**
    * Fuel (diesel consumption based on route distance and generator/engine idling).
    * Consumables per service (shampoo, conditioner, disposable neck strips, sanitizing wipes).
    * Water refilling costs ($~10$–$15$ L per hair wash vs. $25$ L for body shower).
    * Wastewater discharge / municipal treatment disposal fees.
    * Towel laundering and replenishment costs.
    * Utility electricity (shore-power charging costs).

### 5.3 Section 7.3 — Pro Forma Financial Projections & Scenario Modeling
* **3-Year Financial Model across Three Scenarios:**
  * *Conservative Scenario:* 35%–45% daily capacity utilization, lower average price point.
  * *Base-Case Scenario:* 60%–70% capacity utilization, standard pricing.
  * *Optimistic Scenario:* 80%–90% capacity utilization + premium service add-ons / corporate packages.
* **Key Financial Statements to Generate:**
  1. **Pro Forma Income Statement (P&L):** Monthly for Year 1, Annually for Years 2 & 3 (Gross Revenue, Cost of Sales, Gross Margin, Operating Expenses, EBITDA, Depreciation, EBIT, Taxes, Net Profit).
  2. **Pro Forma Cash Flow Statement:** Ensuring positive net cash flow and calculating maximum cash drawdown to prove liquidity solvency.
  3. **Break-Even Analysis (Unit & Revenue):**
     $$\text{Contribution Margin per Service} = \text{Selling Price} - \text{Variable Cost per Service}$$
     $$\text{Daily Break-Even Volume} = \frac{\text{Monthly Fixed Costs}}{\text{Operating Days} \times \text{Contribution Margin per Service}}$$
  4. **Capital Investment Appraisal:**
     * Simple and Discounted **Payback Period**.
     * **Net Present Value (NPV)** over 3–5 years at a realistic Sri Lankan discount rate / cost of capital ($14\%$–$18\%$).
     * **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)**.

### 5.4 Section 7.4 — Startup Funding & Financing Strategy (Session 11)
* **Comparative Evaluation of Financing Options:**
  1. *Founders' Equity / Bootstrapping:* Minimum skin-in-the-game equity contribution.
  2. *Commercial Vehicle Leasing:* Spreading the large initial vehicle cost over 36–60 months rather than an outright cash purchase, preserving initial working capital.
  3. *Concessionary SME / Bank Loans:* Evaluating current Central Bank / commercial bank SME refinancing schemes (e.g., enterprise development loan schemes, women entrepreneurship funds).
  4. *Strategic Corporate Partnerships / CSR Grants:* Sponsoring partner corporate hubs (e.g., wellness programs for corporate tenants).
* **Recommended Capital Structure:**
  * Proposed optimal mix (e.g., 25% Founder Equity + 45% Commercial Equipment/Vehicle Lease + 30% Low-interest SME loan or grant).
  * Debt service schedule and Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) to prove repayment feasibility.
