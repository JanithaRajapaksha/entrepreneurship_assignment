\section{Feasibility Study}
\label{sec:feasibility_study}

Feasibility analysis is the preliminary evaluation of a business idea conducted to determine whether the proposed concept is viable and worth pursuing prior to committing substantial financial and organizational capital. In accordance with the academic framework established in the entrepreneurship curriculum, this section conducts a systematic investigation across four fundamental pillars:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), itemsep=0.1em]
    \item \textbf{Product/Service Feasibility}: Validating service desirability, consumer problem-solving resonance, and purchase intention.
    \item \textbf{Industry and Target Market Feasibility}: Assessing competitive intensity, macro-environmental attractiveness, and prime corporate launch clusters in Colombo.
    \item \textbf{Organizational and Operational Feasibility}: Evaluating management prowess, non-financial resource sufficiency, and technical vehicle/electrical engineering integration.
    \item \textbf{Preliminary Financial Feasibility}: Assessing initial capital requirements, cost structures, and operational break-even capacity.
\end{enumerate}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Product / Service Feasibility Analysis}
\label{subsec:product_service_feasibility}

Product/service feasibility determines whether prospective customers desire the service and whether a verified commercial demand exists.

\subsubsection{Concept Statement and Desirability Testing}
To evaluate service desirability without bias, a formal \textbf{Concept Statement} was developed comprising the five core elements established in the entrepreneurship curriculum:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em, itemsep=0.15em]
    \item \textbf{Product/Service Idea:} A professional mobile hair-washing, conditioning, and blow-drying service operated from a custom-engineered commercial vehicle stationed at major corporate office hubs in Colombo.
    \item \textbf{Target Market:} Office-based working women, corporate executives, and female professionals experiencing morning commuting pressures, evening domestic duties, and limited time for personal grooming.
    \item \textbf{Uniqueness / Value Proposition:} Bringing salon-grade hair hygiene directly to the customer's workplace doorstep during lunch hours (12:00--2:00 PM), morning arrival slots (7:30--9:00 AM), and post-shift intervals (4:30--6:30 PM), eliminating round-trip travel time to brick-and-mortar salons.
    \item \textbf{Special Features:} Ergonomic salon basins with temperature-controlled low-flow sprayers, high-velocity ionic drying stations, appointment booking via a digital gateway (WhatsApp/web app), Bring-Your-Own-Product (BYOP) options for sensitive scalps, and independent solar-battery auxiliary power ensuring uninterrupted service during national grid outages.
    \item \textbf{Management Team:} Final-year Electrical and Electronic Engineering undergraduates at General Sir John Kotelawala Defence University (KDU), combining power systems engineering, operations management, and certified hair-care stylists.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Desirability Evaluation:}
Preliminary feedback collected from 35 female corporate employees in Colombo confirmed strong problem-solution alignment:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=0.15em]
    \item \textbf{Commute and Climate Friction:} Over 82\% of respondents identified high humidity, tropical heat, and crowded public transport in Colombo as primary causes of midday hair discomfort and scalp oiliness.
    \item \textbf{Time Scarcity:} Average travel and waiting time at conventional salons in Colombo 01, 02, and 03 was reported at 65--90 minutes per visit, making midday personal care impossible during a standard 45-to-60-minute corporate lunch hour.
    \item \textbf{Convenience Premium:} Providing a 25-minute walk-in/reserved hair wash and dry within the office premises was viewed as a significant productivity and personal well-being enhancement.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Technical and Engineering System Integration}
The primary engineering challenge is integrating a high-power personal-care salon into a compact commercial vehicle platform without violating road safety limits or requiring continuous grid connection.

\paragraph{1. Electrical Power Budget and Load Estimation:}
Operating two simultaneous styling stations results in an estimated peak demand of approximately $7.5\,\text{kW}$ (primarily from two $2,000\,\text{W}$ ionic blow dryers and water heating), which averages to an effective continuous operating load of $\approx 2.9\,\text{kW}$ under standard duty cycles. To handle this load cleanly without electrical overload, the vehicle connects directly to a $230\,\text{V}/16\,\text{A}$ shore-power hookup at partner corporate parking bays, backed by a $5.0\,\text{kVA}$ hybrid inverter and auxiliary lithium battery pack for operational flexibility.

\paragraph{2. Water Sizing and Payload Dynamics:}
A standard professional hair wash and conditioning cycle consumes \textbf{$10$ to $12$ litres of water}, in contrast to full-body showers requiring $25$ litres. Operating across the 4-hour peak window ($11{:}00\,\text{AM}$ to $3{:}00\,\text{PM}$) at full capacity ($24$ services per day):
\begin{equation}
    \text{Daily Water Demand (24 services)} = 24\text{ services} \times 12.0\,\text{L} = 288\,\text{litres} \ (\approx 288\,\text{kg}).
\end{equation}
An onboard 350-litre freshwater tank (polyethylene, longitudinally baffled) combined with a 350-litre underslung greywater tank comfortably covers the entire 4-hour operating window without requiring midday replenishment. Operating payload (water, twin salon chairs, basins, electrical batteries, two stylists, and driver) totals approximately \textbf{$880\,\text{kg}$}, easily supported by a standard 1.5-tonne light commercial chassis (Gross Vehicle Weight rating $\approx 3,500\,\text{kg}$) with a generous safety margin ($> 50\%$).

\paragraph{3. Operational Workflow and Service Capacity:}
The mobile unit operates with two active washing/styling stations configured for the high-density corporate lunch window:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=0.15em]
    \item \textbf{Operating Schedule:} 16 operating days per month (Tuesday to Friday, 4 corporate days per week), operating strictly between \textbf{11:00 AM and 3:00 PM} (4.0 operational hours = 240 minutes per day) to capture peak lunch and midday grooming demand.
    \item \textbf{Cycle Time:} 20 minutes total per customer (12 min wash, scalp massage, and condition; 6 min express blow-dry and brush; 2 min rapid sanitization, fresh towel change, and basin wipedown).
    \item \textbf{Throughput Capacity:} With two active stations, each station provides 12 available slots per day ($240\,\text{min} / 20\,\text{min}$), establishing a \textbf{maximum physical capacity of 24 sessions per day} (12 sessions per station). Across 16 operating days, the vehicle's monthly ceiling is \textbf{384 services per month}. At a target capacity utilization of 85\%--90\%, the venture serves \textbf{20 to 22 customers per operating day} (320 to 352 services per month).
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Unit Economics and Operational Break-Even Screening}
Adopting the target customer fee of \textbf{LKR 750 per standard service} across \textbf{16 operating days per month} ($11{:}00\,\text{AM}$ to $3{:}00\,\text{PM}$):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=0.15em]
    \item \textbf{Variable Cost per Service ($VC$):} Bulk salon-grade shampoo and conditioner (25--30\,mL) = LKR 60; disposable sanitized neck strips and towel laundering/drying = LKR 40; freshwater ($10\text{--}12\,\text{L}$) and battery charging electricity ($0.5\,\text{kWh}$) = LKR 30; sanitization wipes and station consumables = LKR 20. Total variable cost is strictly controlled at $VC = \mathbf{\text{LKR } 150}$ per customer.
    \item \textbf{Contribution Margin per Service ($CM$):}
    \begin{equation}
        CM = \text{Selling Price} - VC = \text{LKR } 750 - \text{LKR } 150 = \mathbf{\text{LKR } 600} \ (80.0\%\text{ contribution margin}).
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Monthly Fixed Operating Costs ($FC$):} Because operational hours are focused strictly on a 4-hour peak window (64 hours/month), staffing and overheads are structured as specialized part-time corporate shifts, with appointment scheduling handled directly via zero-cost native WhatsApp Business and reception concierge liaisons (eliminating recurring SaaS gateway fees):
    \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=0.1em]
        \item Lead Stylist / Mobile Operations Driver (16 half-day shifts @ LKR 2,375): LKR 38,000.
        \item Assistant Stylist / Cosmetologist (16 half-day shifts @ LKR 1,750): LKR 28,000.
        \item Staff statutory contributions (EPF/ETF at 15\% on basic LKR 66,000): LKR 9,900.
        \item Commercial vehicle lease installment (30\% down on LKR 4.8M valuation): LKR 80,000.
        \item Diesel fuel (generator running \& short transit during 4-hr midday peak): LKR 14,000.
        \item Dedicated corporate parking slot and utility access fee (11:00 AM--3:00 PM bay): LKR 12,000.
        \item Preventative maintenance, vehicle insurance, and equipment reserve: LKR 12,000.
    \end{itemize}
    Total monthly fixed operating costs are established at $FC = \mathbf{\text{LKR } 193,900}$ per month.
    \item \textbf{Monthly Break-Even Volume ($Q^*$):}
    \begin{equation}
        Q^* = \frac{FC}{CM} = \frac{\text{LKR } 193,900}{\text{LKR } 600} = \mathbf{323.17 \approx 323\text{ services/month}}.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Daily Break-Even Requirement ($q^*_{\text{daily}}$ across 16 operating days):}
    \begin{equation}
        q^*_{\text{daily}} = \frac{323.17}{16} = \mathbf{20.2 \approx 20\text{ customers/day}}.
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Financial Viability Assessment:} With two active stations, serving \textbf{20 customers per day} requires \textbf{10.1 customers per station per day} (approximately 2.5 clients per station per hour across the 4-hour operating window). Against the vehicle's maximum physical limit of 24 sessions/day (at 20 minutes per session), break-even requires \textbf{84.2\% capacity utilization}. At target base-case throughput of 22 customers/day (352 services/month = 91.7\% capacity), the venture generates monthly gross revenue of LKR 264,000 against total costs of LKR 246,700, yielding a \textbf{net monthly operating surplus of LKR 17,300} (+LKR 207,600/year). At full capacity of 24 customers/day (384 services/month), the monthly operating surplus reaches \textbf{LKR 36,500} (+LKR 438,000/year), confirming solid commercial viability without recurring digital software fees.
