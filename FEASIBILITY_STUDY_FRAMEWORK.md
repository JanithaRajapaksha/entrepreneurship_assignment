## 1. Executive Overview: What is a Feasibility Analysis?

### 1.1 Definition & Core Purpose
A **feasibility analysis** is the preliminary evaluation of a business idea conducted to determine whether the idea is **viable** and **worth pursuing** prior to committing substantial time, capital, and organizational resources.

```
[Opportunity Recognition / Idea Generation] (Chapter 02)
                   │
                   ▼
     [Feasibility Study / Analysis] (Chapter 03)  <── "Is the idea viable? Worth pursuing?"
                   │
         ┌─────────┴─────────┐
         ▼                   ▼
    [Proceed]           [Drop / Pivot]
         │
         ▼
[Developing the Full Business Plan] (Chapter 04 & 05)
```

### 1.2 Feasibility Study vs. Business Plan
* **Feasibility Study:** An investigative, critical, and objective assessment designed to validate hypotheses and uncover fatal flaws. It asks: *"Will this work? Is it viable across product, market, organization, and finances?"*
* **Business Plan:** An operational and strategic blueprint (typically 25–35 pages) that details *how* a validated business will be structured, launched, marketed, operated, and scaled.

### Pillar 1: Product / Service Feasibility Analysis
Assesses the overall appeal of the proposed product or service before rushing into development.

#### A. Product/Service Desirability
* **Fundamental Validation Questions:**
  * Does it make sense? Is it reasonable?
  * Will consumers get genuinely excited about it?
  * How was the opportunity identified (trend, problem, or marketplace gap)?
  * Is the timing right (is the "window of opportunity" open)?
  * Are there any fatal flaws in its basic concept, design, or user experience?
* **Tool: Concept Test (Concept Statement)**
  A 1-page written statement distributed to 20–50 target customers and experts containing:
  1. Description of the product/service.
  2. Defined target market.
  3. Unique value proposition / point of differentiation.
  4. Special features and benefits.
  5. Profile of the management team.

#### B. Product/Service Demand
* **Step 1: Administer a Buying Intentions Survey**
  * Measures purchase intent using a standard 5-point scale:
    * *Definitely would buy (e.g., benchmark: 50% discount factor applied in forecasting)*
    * *Probably would buy (e.g., benchmark: 10%–20% discount factor applied)*
    * *Might or might not buy*
    * *Probably would not buy*
    * *Definitely would not buy*
* **Step 2: Library, Internet, and "Gumshoe" Research**
  * Fieldwork and face-to-face exploratory interviews ("talk to potential customers!").
  * Observational analysis of user habits and competitor shortcomings.

---

### Pillar 2: Industry and Target Market Feasibility Analysis
Assesses the attractiveness and structural dynamics of the broader industry and the specific niche target market.

#### A. Industry Attractiveness
* Evaluates external macro forces (PESTEL: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal) moving for or against the industry.
* **Key Industry Characteristics:**
  * Age and stage in lifecycle (emerging vs. mature vs. declining).
  * Structure (fragmented vs. concentrated).
  * Growth trajectory (expanding vs. shrinking).
  * Competitive crowding and typical operating margins.
* **Porter's Five Forces Analysis (Chapter 04):**
  1. Threat of New Entrants.
  2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers.
  3. Bargaining Power of Buyers.
  4. Threat of Substitute Products or Services.
  5. Rivalry Among Existing Competitors.

#### B. Target Market Attractiveness
* The objective is to identify a segment that is **large enough to be commercially viable**, yet **small enough to avoid head-to-head competition with established industry giants**.
* **4-Step Market Narrowing Process:**
  1. Map potential customer segments.
  2. Validate assumptions through primary field research.
  3. Narrow down to the most promising, underserved segments.
  4. Select focal target segments offering maximum willingness-to-pay and retention.

---

### Pillar 3: Organizational Feasibility Analysis
Evaluates whether the venture possesses sufficient non-financial competence, capabilities, and resources to execute.

#### A. Management Prowess
* Candid assessment of founders' abilities, requisite passion, and execution capacity.
* Do the skills, professional background, education, and domain expertise of the founders align with the requirements of the venture?

#### B. Resource Sufficiency
* Assessment of the **6 to 12 most critical non-financial resources** required to move the venture forward.
* *Rule of Feasibility:* If critical non-financial resources are unobtainable, the business concept is impractical to proceed in its current configuration.
* **Critical Resources Checklist:**
  * Affordable workspace (office, lab, retail, or industrial space).
  * Reliable equipment and commercial vehicles.
  * Access to specialized contract manufacturers or service suppliers.
  * Key management and frontline personnel availability.
  * Ability to protect Intellectual Property (patents, trademarks, trade secrets).
  * Access to strategic channel and commercial partners.

---

### Pillar 4: Financial Feasibility Analysis
The preliminary financial evaluation based on estimated variables rather than historical accounting records.

#### A. Total Start-Up Cash Needed
* Comprehensive inventory of capital expenditures (CapEx) and pre-launch costs:
  * Property, plant, vehicles, equipment, and structural modifications.
  * Licensing, initial legal compliance, and registration fees.
  * Initial working capital buffer (3 to 6 months of operating cash burn).
  * Contingency reserves (10%–20% buffer for unexpected overruns).

#### B. Financial Performance of Similar Businesses
* "Ethical detective work" through secondary trade reports, public filings, and observational studies (e.g., tracking customer footfall, turn rates, and average spend).

#### C. Overall Financial Attractiveness
* Potential for rapid sales expansion and scalable margins.
* Presence of recurring revenue mechanisms (subscriptions, retainers, repeat visits).
* Predictability of fixed vs. variable cost structures.
* Internal cash-flow generation capability to self-sustain growth without predatory financing.

#### D. Core Mathematical Formulas (Slide 21)
$$\text{Revenue} = \text{Selling Price} \times \text{Expected Sales Volume}$$

$$\text{Total Costs} = \text{Fixed Costs} + \text{Variable Costs}$$

$$\text{Profit} = \text{Revenue} - \text{Total Costs}$$

$$\text{Contribution per Unit} = \text{Selling Price} - \text{Variable Cost per Unit}$$

$$\text{Break-Even Point (Units)} = \frac{\text{Fixed Costs}}{\text{Contribution per Unit}}$$

## 3. Extended Real-World Dimensions (Integrating Engineering & Execution)

When bridging academic theory with rigorous engineering and real-world implementation (as exemplified in `feasibility_study_draft.tex` and Session 06 on Risk Management), a complete study must incorporate:

1. **Technical Feasibility:** Technical design, off-the-shelf vs. custom components, engineering capacity, payload dynamics, energy/utility constraints, and prototyping.
2. **Operational Feasibility:** Daily workflows, front-stage vs. back-stage operations, turnaround cycles, queue management, supply replenishment, and bottleneck identification.
3. **Legal, Regulatory & Environmental Feasibility:** Municipal permits, environmental clearance (e.g., CEA regulations, waste disposal), vehicle roadworthiness (e.g., RMV/gross weight), and health authority (PHI/MOH) standards.
4. **Comprehensive Risk Assessment (Session 06):** Categorization into internal risks (human, equipment, IT, cash flow) and external risks (market, environmental, regulatory), warning signs, and concrete mitigation plans.
5. **Actionable Go / No-Go / Pivot Decision:** Staged milestones to prevent premature capital expenditure before customer validation.

## 4. The Standard Feasibility Study Report Format (Template)

Use the following modular structure when writing or evaluating a Feasibility Study:

```markdown
# Feasibility Study: [Name of Business Concept]

## Executive Summary
- Brief statement of the business opportunity and concept.
- Summary of key findings across all feasibility dimensions.
- Definitive Go / No-Go / Pivot verdict and recommended next steps.

## Section 1: Business Concept & Opportunity Recognition
- Background & context (PESTEL trends driving the opportunity).
- Problem statement & market pain point.
- Proposed solution, value proposition, and unique features.

## Section 2: Product / Service Feasibility
- 2.1 Concept Statement & Concept Testing feedback.
- 2.2 Desirability analysis (timing, flaws, emotional resonance).
- 2.3 Demand validation (Buying Intentions Survey, interviews, field tests).

## Section 3: Technical & Engineering Feasibility
- 3.1 Platform/product design & technical architecture.
- 3.2 Key engineering parameters (materials, weight, power, utilities, capacity).
- 3.3 International and local operational benchmarks.
- 3.4 Key technical risks and engineering solutions.

## Section 4: Industry & Target Market Feasibility
- 4.1 Industry overview, lifecycle stage, and growth trends.
- 4.2 Five Forces competitive intensity analysis.
- 4.3 Target market segmentation, profile, and persona.
- 4.4 Competitor analysis grid and market positioning.

## Section 5: Operational Feasibility
- 5.1 Operating model (front-stage customer experience vs. back-stage operations).
- 5.2 Operating route/schedule, cycle times, and capacity utilization.
- 5.3 Supply chain, replenishment, and waste handling logistics.
- 5.4 Staffing structure, roles, and duty rosters.

## Section 6: Organizational Feasibility
- 6.1 Management team competencies, experience, and leadership prowess.
- 6.2 Evaluation of the 6–12 critical non-financial resources.
- 6.3 Strategic partners, advisers, and external service providers.

## Section 7: Financial Feasibility
- 7.1 Capital expenditure (CapEx) breakdown and start-up budget.
- 7.2 Monthly operating expenditure (OpEx) breakdown.
- 7.3 Revenue model, pricing strategy, and pricing sensitivity.
- 7.4 Contribution margin and Break-Even throughput analysis.
- 7.5 Financial risk exposure and cash flow runway.

## Section 8: Legal, Regulatory & Environmental Feasibility
- 8.1 Required government permits, trade licenses, and business registration.
- 8.2 Public health, safety, and hygiene standards.
- 8.3 Environmental compliance (waste management, emissions, discharge).
- 8.4 Intellectual property and liability considerations.

## Section 9: Risk Management & Mitigation Matrix
- 9.1 Internal risk assessment (human, operational, mechanical, liquidity).
- 9.2 External risk assessment (competition, regulatory changes, public perception).
- 9.3 Structured Risk Mitigation Matrix (Risk | Severity | Mitigation).
- 9.4 Exit strategies and contingency protocols.

## Section 10: Overall Feasibility Conclusion & Action Roadmap
- 10.1 Synthesized Feasibility Scorecard across all dimensions.
- 10.2 Final Go / No-Go / Pivot Recommendation.
- 10.3 Phased Implementation Roadmap (e.g., Phase 1: Survey & Validation, Phase 2: Low-Cost Pilot, Phase 3: Capital Deployment).
```

## 5. Summary Evaluation Scorecard Matrix

A proper feasibility study should conclude with an executive evaluation matrix:

| Feasibility Dimension | Evaluation Criteria | Risk Rating (Low / Med / High) | Status Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Product / Service** | Clear consumer appeal, validated desirability, positive buying intent survey. | Low / Medium / High | Viable / Conditional / Unviable |
| **Technical** | Mature technology, off-the-shelf components, manageable engineering complexity. | Low / Medium / High | Viable / Conditional / Unviable |
| **Industry & Market** | Growing or stable industry, attractive niche segment, manageable competition. | Low / Medium / High | Viable / Conditional / Unviable |
| **Operational** | Smooth daily workflow, capacity bottlenecks resolved, reliable supply chain. | Low / Medium / High | Viable / Conditional / Unviable |
| **Organizational** | Founder competencies match concept; 6–12 critical resources available. | Low / Medium / High | Viable / Conditional / Unviable |
| **Financial** | Manageable startup CapEx, healthy contribution margins, realistic break-even volume. | Low / Medium / High | Viable / Conditional / Unviable |
| **Legal & Environmental** | Full statutory compliance, manageable licensing hurdles, zero illegal discharge. | Low / Medium / High | Viable / Conditional / Unviable |

### The Three-Way Verdict Rule
1. **Proceed (Go):** All dimensions score high viability and acceptable risk. Advance to Business Plan.
2. **Pivot / Redesign:** Idea is desirable, but financial, technical, or regulatory constraints require fundamental changes (e.g., shift from B2C out-of-pocket to B2B employer sponsorship).
3. **Drop (No-Go):** Fatal flaws uncovered (e.g., lack of critical non-financial resources, insurmountable break-even volume, or prohibited regulatory hurdles). Saves valuable capital and time.
