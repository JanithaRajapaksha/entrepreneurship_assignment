## 1. Executive Summary & Purpose of this Document

A peer contribution document titled *Mobile Hair Care – Marketing, Financials & Funding Plan* (@Deepna) was reviewed. While it provides valuable qualitative strategies (property partnerships with Overseas Realty, B2B employer wellness packages, and a staged funding model), its financial calculations fundamentally diverge from our established feasibility study:

* **Deepna's Stance:** Claims a mobile vehicle venture is financially unviable (estimating Rs 8.8M–12.4M CapEx, 15-year payback, and only 9–12 services/day), and proposes abandoning the vehicle for an indoor "portable wash kit" priced at a steep **LKR 1,100 blended**. Deepna explicitly asserts that any price under Rs 1,000 (such as Rs 950) *"fails and loses money"*.
* **Our Feasibility Study Reality:** By utilizing a commercial lease with a 30% down payment on a light commercial truck, operating **two styling stations**, and maintaining realistic salon throughput (20–27 services/day out of a 36-service physical capacity), the mobile vehicle venture is **highly profitable and breaks even at just 21 clients/day at the user-mandated price of LKR 750**.

This document extracts all useful insights from Deepna's plan, reconciles the discrepancies, and re-runs the entire 3-year financial and funding model based strictly on **LKR 750 per service**.

## 2. Key Information & Qualitative Insights Extracted from Deepna's Plan

Deepna's report contains strong operational, marketing, and institutional research that should be adopted into our final report:

### 2.1 Primary Market Survey Findings ($N = 46$ Valid Responses)
* **High Interest:** 78% (36/46) of respondents stated they were likely to try the service; 59% (27/46) rated interest at 4 or 5 out of 5.
* **Price Resistance:** **70% (32/46) stated that affordable pricing must be under LKR 1,000**. *(This directly validates the user's decision to price at LKR 750 rather than Deepna's Rs 1,100!)*.
* **Core Customer Values:** 42 of 46 respondents prioritized **Hygiene**, and 38 prioritized **Privacy**.
* **Time Availability:** 78% of respondents reported having **20 minutes or less** to spare during a workday break.

### 2.2 Multi-Stakeholder Revenue Architecture
Deepna identifies four distinct paying customer segments that can fund the business:
1. **B2C End-Users:** Individual working women purchasing single wash-and-dry services or monthly packages.
2. **B2B Employer Wellness Programs:** Corporate HR departments booking flat-rate "Corporate Grooming Days" (e.g., 10 guaranteed slots for employee welfare/CSR).
3. **Property Management Partnerships:** Partnering with **Overseas Realty** (the property management group that operates **both World Trade Center and Havelock City / Mireka Tower**) to secure parking, water/power hookups, and tenant amenity endorsements.
4. **Brand Product Sponsorships:** Hair-care brands (e.g., Janet, Nature's Secrets, L'Oréal, Keune) providing free shampoo/conditioner products or marketing sponsorship in exchange for sampling and on-vehicle branding.

### 2.3 Solar Energy Reality Check
Deepna's document investigates rooftop solar PV under Sri Lanka's updated CEB/LECO policies (November 2026):
* Total monthly electricity consumption for hair care is modest ($\approx \text{LKR } 5,500/\text{month}$).
* Rooftop solar export tariffs have shifted to "Net Plus" at low buy-back rates (Rs 17–23/unit), making an off-grid solar installation slow to pay back ($> 5$ years).
* **Strategic Consensus:** Design the vehicle to be **"Solar-Ready"** (wiring and space preserved), but rely on direct **$230\text{V} / 16\text{A}$ shore-power hookup** at corporate bays as the primary power source.

### 2.4 Staged De-risking & Investment Gates
Deepna outlines a disciplined "staged investment gate" model:
* **Gate 1:** Pre-sell founding member packs before spending major capital.
* **Gate 2:** Conduct an initial operational pilot at a partner building to verify real appointment cycle times.
* **Gate 3:** Scale to full vehicle/fleet operations only after throughput is proven.

## 4. Redone Financial Model at LKR 750 (Prioritizing Feasibility Study)

### 4.1 Unit Economics Breakdown
* **Selling Price ($P$):** **LKR 750.00**
* **Variable Cost per Service ($VC$):**
  * Salon-grade shampoo and conditioner (bulk 30 mL dispensing): LKR 60.00
  * Sanitized disposable neck strip and towel laundering: LKR 40.00
  * Freshwater ($10\text{--}12\,\text{L}$) and battery charging electricity ($0.5\,\text{kWh}$): LKR 30.00
  * Sanitization wipes, station consumables, and waste handling: LKR 20.00
  * **Total Variable Cost ($VC$):** **LKR 150.00**
* **Contribution Margin ($CM$):**
  $$CM = P - VC = \text{LKR } 750 - \text{LKR } 150 = \mathbf{\text{LKR } 600.00} \ (\mathbf{80.0\%}\text{ margin})$$

### 4.3 Break-Even Analysis at LKR 750
* **Monthly Break-Even Volume ($Q^*$):**
  $$Q^* = \frac{FC}{CM} = \frac{\text{LKR } 310,000}{\text{LKR } 600} = \mathbf{517\text{ services / month}}$$
* **Daily Break-Even Requirement across 25 Operating Days ($q^*_{\text{daily}}$):**
  $$q^*_{\text{daily}} = \frac{517}{25} \approx \mathbf{20.7 \approx 21\text{ customers / day}}$$
* **Station Utilization:** Across two styling stations, 21 customers/day requires **only 10.5 customers per station per day** ($\approx 1.3$ customers per station per hour).
* **Capacity Utilization:** Relative to the vehicle's maximum physical capacity of 38 sessions/day (25-min cycle over 8 hours), break-even occurs at **55.3% capacity utilization**.
